Perceiving their status as threatened by advances in gender equality and a widespread economic and
existential precarity, young men (18-30 years old) today feel increasingly insecure about their masculine
worth. To address such perceptions of precarious manhood, many find support in traditional
masculinity norms emphasizing dominance, toughness, and stoicism. Such norms also echo widely-
held meritocratic norms glorifying self-reliance and competition. Currently, we know little about how
young men use such traditional masculinity and meritocratic norms to address their precarious manhood.
This project introduces Meritocratic Masculinities (MMs) as those identities wherein young men address
their precarious manhood by cultivating a view of the self (i.e., self-schema) as worthy based on shared
meritocratic and traditionally masculine norms (i.e., competition, self-reliance, dominance, stoicism).
Such self-schema is further complemented by an emotional expression emphasizing confidence and
determination, but also anger and isolation, as emotions that reject vulnerability and emphasize control. To study young men’s adoption of such MMs self-schema and emotions, this project particularly
focuses on their most consumed social media and music platforms, namely TikTok, YouTube, and Spotify. With around 75% of Belgian youth using these social media daily and 64% of them listening to
music for over 2 hours every day, these platforms likely play a central role for young men’s identities.
While we currently lack a systematic analysis of MMs messages in popular media, my PhD dissertation
hints at the wide presence of meritocratic and traditional masculinity norms in English-language music
lyrics. Moreover, recent research on the so-called “manosphere” shows that traditional masculinity
and meritocratic ideals of the “self-made man” are increasingly popular on social media platforms. By
glorifying competition, dominance, self-reliance, and stoicism, popular media frequently depicts norms
related to MMs, hinting at their wide presence in the media content daily consumed by young men.
Currently, research about the links between media uses and young men’s gender socialization is
limited or inconsistent. The documentation of inconsistent media effects is somewhat typical in media
psychology research and thus exemplary of a more general scientific challenge in this field: to explain
why and how media effects operate. To address this impasse, the current project introduces a new model
called the Resonant Experiences with Media (REM), which refines scholarly understanding of how
audiences learn from media that reflect their own biographical questioning. The model will be developed
and tested using a mixed-method approach (interviews, content analyses, and ESM) among Belgian young
men, and by using social media and music as key case studies. Overall, this project breaks new theoretical
ground in three ways: 1) by being the first to conceptualize and empirically study the concept of MMs to
explain how young men address their precarious manhood; 2) by being the first to develop a theoretical
model that integrates dual sub-processes of resonance (i.e., cognitive and emotional) to explain the role
of media for young men’s MMs self-schema and emotions; and 3) by clarifying everyday multimodal and
multiplatform experiences among a heterogeneous group of young men. From a societal perspective,
these results will further 4) address longstanding concerns about shifting masculinity identities and the socializing role of popular media in youth’s everyday lives. It will provide crucial insights for
researchers and practitioners, developed into an evidence-based media literacy trainings to help youth
navigate masculinity messages in today’s media ecosystems.
